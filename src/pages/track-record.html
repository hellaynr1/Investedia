Track record

When an article implies an asset will rise or fall, we check what it did over the next 24 hours. Every call is kept, hits and misses alike.

How a call is counted

  • The call. The direction (up or down) an article implies for an asset that has a market price. Mixed and unclear articles are not calls.
  • The check. For live-recorded calls: the price change from the first price recorded after the article to the first price recorded at least 24 hours later (within 48 hours, so weekends and market holidays are handled honestly). Stocks are delayed 15–30 minutes.
  • Older articles. Articles from before live recording are judged on daily closes instead: the close of the session on the article's day to the close of the next session. They are marked "daily close". Every qualifying article is counted; none are chosen by result.
  • Hit, miss, no clear move. A hit moved at least 0.3% the way the article implied. A miss moved at least 0.3% the other way. Anything between is "no clear move" and is left out of the hit rate.
  • Hit rate = hits ÷ (hits + misses). We show it only after 50 judged calls; before that a percentage would mean little.

Article directions are written with the help of AI from each source's reporting. Past results do not predict future ones. Investedia is media, not advice: nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.
